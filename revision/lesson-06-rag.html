PART 1 · THE IDEA IN 60 SECONDS

The model hasn't read your PDF. RAG hands it the right pages.

An LLM only knows what it was trained on, and it forgets everything between requests. It has never seen velmora_guide.pdf. Retrieval-Augmented Generation fixes that at question time: retrieve the few passages most related to the question, augment the prompt with them, then let the model generate an answer from them.

The model doesn't learn anything and its weights don't change. You only change what it's looking at when it answers.

Analogy: an open-book exam. The model is a smart student who never read your book. Before each question, a librarian (vector search) finds the three most relevant pages and puts them on the desk. The student answers from those pages, and is told to say "it's not in here" when it isn't.

Why not paste the whole PDF every time?

It may not fit

Context windows have a limit, and "all documents" mode can mean ten PDFs at once.

You pay for it on every question

Cost grows with input tokens. Three chunks are about 2,000 tokens; a whole book is far more, and you'd send it every time.

It gets slower

A bigger prompt means a slower first word. Users notice.

Answers get worse

Long, noisy contexts make models miss facts, especially facts buried in the middle.

The honest caveat: for one short document, a long-context model with the whole text can work fine. RAG is a tradeoff that pays off as data grows, not a law.

RAG has two halves

Offlineat upload (lesson 4)
Parse PDF→Chunk ~500 words→Embed each chunk→Store in pgvector
Onlineevery question (this lesson)
Embed question→Find closest chunks→Put them in the prompt→Generate + stream→Save

    PART 6 · WHAT BIT YOU

    Your own bugs and numbers. Interviewers love these.

    0.002

    The follow-up coin flip

    "and who founded it?" found the right chunk by 0.450 vs 0.452. The agent's rewritten query got it at 0.366. Search on the raw message can't use history.

    4 → 11

    A test set that was too easy

    With a 4-chunk PDF, top 3 was most of the document, so the baseline passed by accident. You grew it to 11 chunks with "built by" and "founded by" traps.

    8/9 → 9/9

    Classic vs agent

    Same 9 questions. The agent fixed the follow-up, answered "which documents?" with listDocuments, and split a two-part question into two searches.

    "Paris!"

    Ungrounded answers

    "What's the capital of France?" got answered from general knowledge. Now the rules say: only answer from the documents, and say so when they don't cover it.

    status = 'ready'

    Half a PDF

    Once embedding moved to a queue (Phase 6), a processing document had some chunks stored. Search filters on ready documents, or answers come from half a file.

    user_id

    History can leak

    getRecentMessages returns nothing to the client, but it feeds the prompt. Unscoped, someone else's sessionId would let the model answer from their chat.

    2 calls

    The price of the agent

    A document question costs 2 model calls (search, then answer). Answers took 5–11 s vs 4–8 s on the classic path that day.

    top 3

    Meta questions

    "Which documents do I have?" can't be answered by searching chunks: no chunk says that. That needed a tool, not a better prompt.

    PART 7 · INTERVIEW DRILL

    27 questions you could get. Answer out loud first, then open.

    Say it in 60 seconds: "Walk me through what happens when a user asks a question."

    "The API checks the JWT first, so everything after is scoped to that user. The question is embedded with Gemini into 3072 numbers. Postgres with pgvector compares that vector against the stored chunk vectors of the user's ready documents and returns the three closest by cosine distance. Those chunks, the last eight messages of the conversation and the question go to the model, with rules: answer only from this material, and say so when it isn't there. The reply streams back over SSE and both messages are saved.

    Since Phase 7 there's an agent in the middle: the model writes its own search query, so a follow-up like 'and who founded it?' becomes 'who founded the Lantern Festival' before the search. On my test set that took it from 8 of 9 to 9 of 9."

    Each answer opens with Say this first: the 10-second version to lead with. Then the fuller explanation, an example, how it applies in DocMind, the likely follow-up, and the wrong answer to avoid. Say the short answer, then go deeper only if they ask.
