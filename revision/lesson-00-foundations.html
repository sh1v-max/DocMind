PART 2 · RULES VS LEARNING

Normal code is rules you write. Machine learning is rules learned from examples.

Traditional programming

Rules you write+Data→Answers

A spam filter as code: if subject.includes("WIN $$$") → spam. Works until spammers write "W1N". You keep adding rules forever.

Machine learning

Data+Answers (labels)→Training→Model = learned rules

Show it 100,000 emails already marked spam / not spam. Training finds the patterns itself, including ones you'd never think to write.

A model is the result of training: a function with learned numbers (parameters) inside. You give it a new input, it gives a prediction. Use ML when the rules are too fuzzy to write by hand (language, images, spam); use normal code when they aren't (tax maths, permissions).

PART 3 · TRAIN A MODEL YOURSELF

Training is a loop: guess, measure how wrong, adjust a little, repeat

The smallest possible model: a straight line, y = w × x + b, with two parameters, w (slope) and b (starting height). The task: learn the line that fits the dots. Both start at 0, so the first guess is terrible. Press Step to watch one round of the loop, then Train 100 steps.

1 · Predictrun the line on every x
2 · Losshow wrong, on average
3 · Gradientwhich way to nudge
4 · Updatenudge w and b a little
0steps
0loss (lower = better)
0w (slope)
0b (height)
Press Step to run the loop once.
Analogy: finding the bottom of a valley in fog. You can't see the lowest point (the best w and b), but you can feel which way the ground slopes under your feet (the gradient). Take a step downhill, feel again, repeat. The learning rate is your step size: too small and it takes forever, too big and you jump over the valley. Try 0.55.

The same loop trains an LLM

  • 2 parameters → billions. Same idea, just many more numbers to nudge.
  • 12 dots → trillions of tokens of text, shown in small batches; one pass over all the data is an epoch.
  • Loss = how surprised the model was by the real next token. Every wrong guess nudges the weights so the right token gets a bit more likely next time.
  • Backpropagation is just the efficient way to work out the gradient for every weight at once in a network with many layers.
PART 4 · TYPES OF LEARNING

Four ways a model can learn, and all of them are used to build an LLM

Supervised

Learn from examples with the right answer attached (labels). Spam / not spam, house price from past sales.

Unsupervised

No labels. Find structure on its own: group similar customers, spot unusual transactions.

Self-supervised

The labels come from the data itself: hide the next word, then predict it. No humans needed, so it scales to the whole internet.

Reinforcement

Learn by trying things and getting a reward or penalty. Game AIs, and tuning chatbots toward answers people prefer.

PART 5 · OVERFITTING

A model that memorises its training data fails on new data

Blue dots are training data (the model sees them). Amber rings are test data (held back, to check it on unseen examples). Raise the model's complexity and watch: the curve hits the blue dots better and better, but past a point it gets worse on the amber ones. It started learning the noise instead of the pattern.

0error on training data
0error on test data

Blue line: training error. Amber line: test error. The dot marks your setting.

Underfitting

Too simple to capture the pattern. Bad on training and test data. A straight line through a wave.

Good fit

Learns the pattern, ignores the noise. Similar error on training and test data. This is what generalisation means.

Overfitting

Memorises the training data, noise included. Great on training, bad on test. Like a student who memorised last year's answers.

Train, validation, test

Split your data three ways. Train (~70–80%): the model learns from it. Validation (~10–15%): you check it while choosing settings. Test (~10–15%): touched once at the end, for an honest score. If you tune on the test set, your score is no longer honest.

Common fixes for overfitting: more data, a simpler model, stopping training early, regularisation (a penalty for over-complicated fits), dropout.

PART 6 · INSIDE A TRANSFORMER

What happens to your text inside the model, in five steps

Every modern LLM (Gemini, GPT, Claude, Llama) is a transformer. You don't need the math; you need this picture. Lesson 1 zooms in on step 5.

The lighthouse was built …
1 / 5
TypeWhat it's good atExamples
Decoder-onlyGenerating text, one token at a time. What chat models are.GPT, Gemini, Claude, Llama
Encoder-onlyUnderstanding text: classification, search, embeddings.BERT and the embedding models built on that idea
Encoder-decoderTurning one text into another: translation, summarising.T5, the original 2017 transformer

Why transformers replaced older models (RNNs): an RNN reads one word at a time and slowly forgets what came long before. A transformer reads all the words at once, so it trains in parallel on GPUs and links distant words directly. That's what made huge models practical.

"Open-weight" means you can download the trained weights. It isn't always "open source": the training data and code usually aren't public, and licences vary.

How much memory does a model need? quick estimate

Each parameter is a number stored in some precision. Weights memory ≈ parameters × bytes per parameter. Quantization stores them with fewer bits, so the model fits on smaller hardware, with a small quality loss.

Precision
14 GBfor the weights
~17 GBto actually serve it (rough +20%)

The +20% is a rough allowance for the working memory a model needs while answering. Real numbers depend on context length and traffic.

Does it fit?

Customising a model

Prompting and RAG

No training at all. Change what you send. Always try this first; it's what DocMind does.

Fine-tuning

Train an existing model a bit more on your own examples, to change its behaviour: format, tone, a narrow task. Bad at adding facts.

LoRA bonus

Cheap fine-tuning: freeze the model and train small add-on pieces instead, often under 1% of the parameters. Fits on one GPU.

Distillation bonus

Train a small "student" model to copy a big "teacher" model's answers. Faster and cheaper, a bit less capable.

Words you should recognise

GPU

A chip that does thousands of simple maths operations at once. Training and running neural networks is mostly that, so GPUs (and Google's TPUs) do the work.

Multimodal

A model that takes more than text: images, audio, video, PDFs. Gemini is one.

Mixture of Experts

A big model split into many "expert" parts, where only a few run for each token. Lots of knowledge, less compute per answer.

Scaling laws

The finding that models get predictably better with more parameters, data and compute. Why models kept getting bigger.

Benchmarks

Standard test sets used to compare models. Useful, but your own small eval on your own task matters more.

Hyperparameters

Settings you choose, not learned: learning rate, batch size, number of layers. Parameters are what training learns.

PART 8 · WHERE DOCMIND FITS

You never trained a model. That's normal for an AI backend role.

Everything DocMind does is inference on models someone else trained. The backend job is the part around the model: what goes in, checking what comes out, storing, scaling and securing it. Knowing how training works is what lets you explain why the model behaves the way it does.

2 GB vs 512 MB

Why an API, not self-hosting

Even a tiny 1B-parameter model needs about 2 GB at 16-bit. Render's free tier gives the whole server 512 MB, and has no GPU. Calling Gemini was the only realistic choice, and it's free.

Closed model

The trade-off you accepted

Gemini Flash-Lite through the API: strong and free, but your data goes to Google, you live with its rate limits (15 per minute), and the -latest alias can change under you.

Learned meaning

Embeddings are a trained model's output

gemini-embedding-001 learned, during training, to put similar texts close together. Your search and router just use that. Lesson 2.

No fine-tuning

RAG instead

DocMind adds knowledge by putting chunks in the prompt, not by training. New PDFs are searchable seconds after upload, and per-user privacy is a database filter.

Probabilistic

Why you validate outputs

A trained model produces likely text, not guaranteed text. That's why the quiz JSON goes through Zod and the classifier has a safe fallback.

52 labelled messages

You did build an eval

Your router eval is a small labelled test set, the same idea as Part 5's test data: measure on examples the system wasn't tuned on.

PART 9 · INTERVIEW DRILL

26 questions you could get. Answer out loud first, then open.

Say it in 60 seconds: "Explain AI, ML and deep learning."

"AI is the broad goal of making computers do things that seem intelligent. Machine learning is one way to get there: instead of writing rules, you show a model lots of examples and it learns the rules itself, by repeatedly guessing, measuring how wrong it was and adjusting its parameters. Deep learning is machine learning with neural networks of many layers, which is what made language and images workable. Generative AI uses those networks to create new text or images, and LLMs are the text kind, trained to predict the next token. In my project I don't train models; I build the backend around one, with RAG, validation and streaming."

Each answer opens with Say this first: the 10-second version to lead with. Questions marked bonus go beyond what most intern interviews ask; skip them if you're short on time.
